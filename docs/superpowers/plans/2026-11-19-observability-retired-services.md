# Retired services and cutover closure

This adds an explicit cleanup acceptance check to the November 19 observability parity plan. Production cutover and later Phoenix retirement each require owner approval.

1. Before cutover, record the expected generated service set and the exact legacy projects, container IDs, user units, listeners, writers and volume mappings that will be replaced. Identify which volumes are retained for rollback or history and how they can be restored.
2. After the approved cutover passes service, history and ingestion checks, acquire the migration ownership fence. Before each removal, re-read the engine identity, full container ID, project and service labels, current generation and mount identities. Reconcile the relevant unit fragment or enablement link with its recorded path and verified owner. Unexpected or ambiguous ownership blocks that operation and the cutover gate. Pass the exact attested container ID or unit identity to each removal. Check the remaining running and stopped inventory, listener ownership, units, timers and single active writer against the approved service set.
3. Phoenix remains an expected service during the 30 complete days of parallel comparison. After equivalent Grafana views and counts, owner confirmation and separate retirement approval, require a working history read path independent of the retiring container. Before removing it, read representative retained records through that path while the old listener is unavailable, reconcile expected IDs and counts, and exercise the rollback mapping and single-writer handoff using a verified disposable history copy. A failed history or rollback check blocks removal and preserves the original history volume. Record the rollback artifacts and owner-approved retention policy. Then remove Phoenix from desired membership, stop its exporter route, and remove its exact owned container and any dedicated unit and listener. Persist the retired generation across restarts. Rerun the independent history read after removal and restart.
4. Rehearse cleanup, Phoenix retirement and rollback with disposable projects. Seed running and stopped superseded containers, a foreign lookalike and a candidate with ambiguous ownership. Change a candidate's ID or owner between initial discovery and removal. Verify detections, exact removed IDs, retained volume identities and the unknown-or-changed-owner gate. Prove the foreign, ambiguous and replaced candidates are untouched. After retirement, compare generated membership and running and stopped runtime inventory, then exercise old manual entry points, obsolete units and a boot-equivalent restart with the new owner active, stopped and the old port free. Require zero unauthorized recreation or duplicate intake. Exercise the same old entry points during rollback and after restoring the recorded generation. Reconcile generated and runtime membership and offline history before restoring the former single writer. Verify previous service intake, exactly one publisher and no orphan containers after rollback.
